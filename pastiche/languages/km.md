Khmer notes:
- Romanization convention: `h` after a consonant marks aspiration (a puff of air), never a
  fricative. `ph` = p as in "pot" (NOT f — Khmer has no native /f/), `kh` = hard k, `th` = t
  as in Thailand.
- Khmer contrasts aspirated/unaspirated; English contrasts voiced/voiceless. English ears
  therefore hear unaspirated t/p/k as d/b/g. The ear is not wrong; the romanization writes
  t/p/k anyway. Say so when the learner reports hearing the "wrong" consonant.
- The palatal nasal ញ is romanized `ny` here (nyam, khnyom). Official transliterations write
  it `nh` — same sound.
- Vowel length is phonemic. Double the letter when long (baay).
- Consonant clusters stack in the script: the second consonant becomes a subscript (coeng)
  tucked under the first — ខ្ញុំ is kh with a small ញ beneath.
- Final consonants are unreleased; English ears drop them entirely (toch heard as "too").
- When a native speaker's pronunciation and the romanization disagree, the speaker is right.
  Record the heard form alongside the standard one.
